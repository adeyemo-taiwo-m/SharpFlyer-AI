## 2. Executive Summary and Product Vision

### 2.1 What it is

SharpFlyer AI is a web-based tool that lets businesses create professional promotional flyers in minutes. A user picks a professionally designed template from a curated gallery, enters their business details (headline, offer, price, contact number, logo, product photo), and receives a finished flyer sized for WhatsApp Status, Instagram, Facebook, and print.

### 2.2 What it does

- Adapts a chosen template to the user’s content while preserving the design’s structure.
- Uses AI to write or improve headlines, offer wording, and calls to action.
- Uses AI to help fit content into layouts and prepare product images (for example, background removal).
- Renders all critical information (prices, phone numbers, business name, logo) from structured data, never from an image model, so it is always accurate.
- Exports high-resolution PNG and JPG files in the right dimensions for each platform.
- Later: generates entirely new designs from a text prompt.

### 2.3 Who it serves

Small and medium-sized Nigerian businesses that advertise regularly on social media. The initial focus is solar and inverter companies, who promote packages, prices, and offers frequently.

### 2.4 The problem it solves

These businesses need fresh promotional graphics often, but designers are slow and costly, Canva requires design skill and time, generic AI image generators misspell text and mangle prices, and reusing old flyers gets repetitive.

### 2.5 Value proposition

> **“Sharp flyers, sharp sharp.”** Pick a design, add your details, and get a professional flyer in minutes, with your price and phone number always correct.

### 2.6 What makes it different

1. **Curated starting point.** Users choose a design they already like instead of prompting and hoping.
2. **Reliable business data.** Prices, numbers, and logos are rendered from structured fields, not generated by an image model.
3. **Built for recurring promotion.** Saved brand profiles and a repeatable workflow make the second and tenth flyer faster than the first.
4. **Local fit.** Naira pricing, Nigerian payment methods, WhatsApp-first formats, and templates designed for categories Nigerian businesses actually advertise.

These differentiators are hypotheses \[H\] and must be validated against Canva, local designers, and generic AI tools.

### 2.7 Product principles

1. **Accuracy over novelty.** A correct price on a good design beats a beautiful image with the wrong number.
2. **Fewest steps to a finished flyer.** A first-time user should export a flyer in under 5 minutes without a tutorial.
3. **Constrained, not blank.** Guided choices beat an empty canvas.
4. **Cost-aware AI.** Every AI call must justify its cost.
5. **Mobile first.** Most target users will operate from a phone.

## 3. Problem Statement and Evidence

### 3.1 The user’s pain point

A business owner has an offer to promote today and needs a good-looking flyer within the hour. Each current option has a significant drawback.

| Current option | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hire a designer | High quality | Costs money, needs briefing, revisions, and waiting time |
| Canva and similar tools | Flexible, large template library | User must find a template, edit every element, and have some design sense; mobile editing is fiddly |
| Generic AI image generators | Attractive visuals | Unreliable text, wrong prices and numbers, inconsistent layout, no brand memory |
| Reuse old flyers | Fast | Repetitive, needs manual editing, looks stale |

### 3.2 When and why it occurs

- A new package, price change, promotion, or seasonal sale is launched.
- The business wants to post on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, and Facebook the same day.
- The owner or a staff member has no design skills or time.

### 3.3 Frequency and impact

- Frequency: assumed weekly to monthly for active advertisers \[H\].
- Impact: slow or low-quality promotion, lost sales opportunities, money spent on designers, and inconsistent brand appearance.

### 3.4 Evidence status

| Statement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Nigerian SMEs regularly post promotional graphics on social media | \[H\] to confirm in interviews |
| Solar and inverter companies promote packages and prices often | \[H\] |
| Target users find Canva too time-consuming for frequent flyers | \[H\] |
| Generic AI tools make text and price errors | \[V\] widely observed behavior of image models; confirm with test prompts |
| Businesses will pay a recurring fee for faster flyers | \[U\] must be proven by paid pilot |

## 4. Target Users and Needs

### 4.1 Primary segment (MVP)

**Solar and inverter businesses in Nigeria** (installers, resellers, distributors).

- Promote packages (for example, inverter plus battery bundles) with clear prices.
- Depend on product images and visible contact details.
- Often run offers tied to market events, power-supply news, and seasons.

### 4.2 Secondary segments (post-MVP)

- Fashion and retail stores promoting new stock and sales.
- Real estate agents advertising listings.
- Restaurants, logistics companies, event organizers, and service providers.

### 4.3 Personas

**Persona 1: Chidi, owner of a solar installation company**

- Goals: post a new package flyer weekly; look credible; get inquiries on WhatsApp.
- Frustrations: designers take days; Canva edits take too long on his phone.
- Environment: Android phone, mobile data with variable speed.
- Technical ability: comfortable with WhatsApp and Instagram, not with design tools.

**Persona 2: Amaka, social media manager for a retail brand**

- Goals: produce several sale and arrival flyers per week in a consistent brand style.
- Frustrations: repetitive editing, brand inconsistency across posts.
- Environment: laptop and phone.
- Technical ability: moderate; knows Canva.

**Persona 3: Tunde, sales rep at an inverter reseller**

- Goals: quickly create a flyer for a specific customer deal or WhatsApp broadcast.
- Frustrations: depends on the marketing team for every design.
- Environment: phone only.

### 4.4 Buyer versus user

In most target businesses the owner is both buyer and user. In larger ones the buyer is the owner or marketing head while the user is a staff member. The MVP supports a single user per account. Team seats are out of scope.

### 4.5 Jobs to be done

1. When I have a new offer, help me publish a professional flyer today without hiring anyone.
2. When I advertise regularly, help me keep my brand look consistent without redoing the work each time.
3. When I only have my phone, let me finish the whole job there.
4. When I do not know what to write, suggest wording that sells.

### 4.6 Constraints and usage environment

- Primarily mobile browsers (Chrome on Android, Safari on iOS); desktop secondary.
- Variable network quality: pages and previews must stay usable on slow connections.
- Users are comfortable in English, with Nigerian Pidgin tone sometimes preferred for copy \[H\].
- Common payment methods: card, bank transfer, USSD, and bank apps through a local payment gateway.

## 5. Product Goals and Success Metrics

### 5.1 Business objectives

1. Prove that solar and inverter businesses will pay for the product.
2. Prove that paying users come back to create more flyers.
3. Maintain positive gross margin after AI, hosting, storage, and payment costs.

### 5.2 User outcomes

- Create and export a usable flyer quickly.
- Trust that prices and contact details are correct.
- Reuse saved brand details to make later flyers faster.

### 5.3 Key performance indicators (proposed targets)

| KPI | Definition | Target (first 90 days after launch) | Collection method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activation rate | Signed-up users who export at least one flyer within 24 hours | 40% or higher | Event tracking |
| Time to first export | Median time from signup to first export | Under 5 minutes | Event timestamps |
| Weekly repeat creators | Users who export in 2 or more different weeks | 25% of activated users | Event tracking |
| Free-to-paid conversion | Activated users who purchase a plan or credits | 5% or higher | Payment records |
| Paid pilot conversions | Pilot businesses that pay | At least 10 of 30 contacted | Manual log plus payment records |
| Export success rate | Render requests that produce a valid file | 99% or higher | Server logs |
| Information accuracy | Exports where price, phone, and name match input exactly | 100% | Automated validation |
| Gross margin per paying user | Revenue minus AI, hosting, storage, and payment costs | Positive, with 60% or higher goal | Cost tracking |
| User satisfaction | Share of exports rated “good” in post-export prompt | 80% or higher | In-app rating |

### 5.4 Guardrail metrics

- AI cost per exported flyer must stay under the agreed ceiling (to be set once provider pricing is known) \[U\].
- Regeneration rate: if the average user regenerates more than 3 times per export, quality is too low.
- Support requests per 100 exports must not grow as volume grows.
- Free-tier abuse: accounts generating above the fair-use threshold are flagged.

### 5.5 Pre-build validation gate

Before full development, complete the validation sprint described in Section 15. Proceed only if at least some businesses pay or give credible purchase commitments, and interviewees say they would use it again.

### 6.2 Explicitly excluded (Won’t Have this release)

- Full drag-and-drop canvas editor.
- Unlimited free generations.
- Team collaboration, roles, and approval flows.
- Video, animation, or GIF generation.
- Large template library (hundreds of designs).
- Exposing multiple AI models to customers.
- Auto-publishing to social media or scheduling.
- Campaign management and advanced brand analytics.
- Scraping or copying flyers from social platforms or competitors.
- Native mobile apps (the MVP is a mobile-optimized web app).

### 6.3 Future releases

| Release | Candidate scope |
| --- | --- |
| v1.1 | Prompt-to-design (Mode B) wider release, natural-language edits, more templates and industries |
| v1.2 | Team seats, shared brand kits, template favorites, WhatsApp-share shortcut |
| v2.0 | Campaign sets (multiple formats and variations), scheduling and posting integrations, installable PWA or native app |

### 6.4 Platform and device scope

- Responsive web app. Supported: latest two versions of Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge; Android Chrome; iOS Safari.
- Minimum supported viewport: 360px width.

### 6.5 Known limitations

- Templates control layout, so users cannot freely move arbitrary elements in the MVP.
- Very long text may be shortened or resized to fit; the system warns the user.
- AI-generated copy and imagery may need human review before publishing.

### 7.2 Navigation hierarchy

- **Public header:** Logo, Templates (preview), Pricing, Log in, Get started.
- **App navigation (bottom bar on mobile, sidebar on desktop):** Home, Templates, My Flyers, Brand, Billing.
- **Persistent element:** credit balance indicator.

### 7.3 Primary user flow: create a flyer from a template (Mode A)

1. User lands on the home page and taps **Get started**.
2. Signs up (phone OTP, email, or Google) and sees a short, skippable onboarding that asks business type and optionally saves brand details.
3. Opens the **Template gallery** pre-filtered by the business type (for example, Solar).
4. Previews a template and taps **Use this design**.
5. Fills in the **details form**: headline, offer/body, price, contact number, business name, logo, product image, and output format. AI can suggest headlines and calls to action.
6. Taps **Generate flyer**. The system validates input, renders the flyer, and shows a preview.
7. Reviews the preview. Options: edit fields, change colors from a palette, regenerate AI-assisted parts, or switch format.
8. Taps **Export** and downloads PNG or JPG. The flyer is saved to **My Flyers**.

### 7.4 Alternative flows

- **Reuse a previous flyer:** My Flyers, then Duplicate, edit details, export.
- **Resize an existing flyer:** open a flyer, choose a different format, export.
- **Create from AI prompt (Mode B):** describe the flyer, supply details, review generated designs, pick one, refine, export.
- **Out of credits:** blocked at generate or export with a clear prompt to buy credits or upgrade; draft is preserved.
- **Returning user:** the dashboard offers “Create another” using saved brand details pre-filled.

### 7.5 Entry and exit points

- Entry: landing page, direct links shared on WhatsApp, referral links (future), template deep links.
- Exit/success: flyer exported; flyer shared through the system share sheet (Should Have).

### 7.6 Decision points and branching

| Decision point | Branch |
| --- | --- |
| New or returning user | Onboarding or dashboard |
| Template or AI prompt | Mode A gallery or Mode B prompt |
| Required fields valid | Proceed or show inline errors |
| Credits available | Generate or show purchase prompt |
| Render success | Preview or retryable error |
| Satisfied with preview | Export or edit/regenerate |

### 7.7 Authentication and onboarding

- Sign-in options: phone number with OTP, email with magic link or password, Google.
- First-run onboarding (three screens at most): business type, business name and contact, optional logo and colors. Skippable.
- Account verification required before export to limit abuse.
